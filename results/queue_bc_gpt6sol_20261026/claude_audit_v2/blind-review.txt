## 3. Findings and limitations

1. **Cycles and simulator executions carry no independent information.** Per-execution cycle increments are 10, 14, 18, 22 in every run, i.e. 6 seed cycles + 4k replayed actions, giving cumulative 10/24/42/64.
   - Every run used the maximum four actions every round.
   - Executions always equal the round number.
   - Both metrics are deterministic transforms of `first_target_round`. Reporting them alongside rounds triple-counts a single integer outcome taking values 2, 3 or 4.

2. **Ceiling effects.**
   - Line, branch and expression coverage saturate in all 12 runs.
   - Toggle coverage plateaus at exactly 344/408 (84.3%) in all 12 runs, with no run exceeding it. The remaining 64 toggle points are plausibly unreachable or constant bits; this needs per-bit inspection.
   - Endpoint coverage cannot discriminate the arms. Only time-to-target can, and it is a coarse ordinal with three of 12 runs at the R4 cap.

3. **Pre-feedback arm imbalance in round 1.**
   - Round-1 tokens are systematically higher for Y: about +1,531 in the original (means 39,989 vs 41,520) and about +1,600 in the variant (41,367 vs 42,967), with tight within-arm spread.
   - Round-1 coverage also differs by arm: Y produces the 33-line profile in 4 of 6 runs, X in 0 of 6.
   - If round 1 precedes any feedback, the arms already differ in prompt or context. Some downstream differences may then stem from this, not from the intended manipulation.
   - I draw no inference about which label carries semantic feedback.

4. **Token cost to target conflates stopping round with per-call size.** Y's lower to-target means come entirely from reaching 41/41 earlier. At equal rounds Y's cumulative tokens are typically higher, and total round-4 spend is mixed in direction.
   - "Line covered at stop" is misleading: runs did not stop at target. It is a post-hoc first-hit metric.

5. **Suspicious duplication across versions and sessions.**
   - Variant X R1–R2 toggle/line/branch trajectories are identical to original X for every repetition (306→343, 258→295, 258→311).
   - Exact tuples recur across arms and versions, for example (12, 26, 258) and (13, 33, 240).
   - This may reflect a low-diversity, near-deterministic stimulus space. It may also be a join or copy error. Either way, it contradicts treating sessions as rich independent samples and should be checked against raw logs.

6. **Uncertainty and power.**
   - With 3 vs 3 unpaired independent sessions (repetition indices are not meaningful pairs), the smallest achievable two-sided exact permutation p is 0.10, so the design cannot support significance claims.
   - Observed differences are about one run's shift by one round.
   - The two versions are one IP, so they are not independent replications.

7. **Cross-version comparison is not supported.**
   - Expression totals differ (16 vs 40), so the "equivalent" variant is structurally different.
   - Equal line/branch/toggle totals do not imply a location-for-location correspondence of counters.
   - Equivalence itself is not demonstrated in the packet.

8. **No checker signal.** `distinct_defect_count = 0` everywhere. Bug-finding cannot be assessed, and a vacuous checker cannot be distinguished from a correct one.

9. **Unverifiable provenance.**
   - SHA-256 hashes are listed but the CSV bytes are absent, so I cannot confirm the JSON rows match the hashed files.
   - Feedback isolation, checker validity and rewrite soundness require raw transcripts, coverage databases and replay.

## Verdict

The packet is arithmetically internally consistent: all target costs reproduce exactly.

However, it does not show a credible X/Y difference in either RTL version:
- Endpoint coverage is identical and ceilinged, including the 344/408 toggle plateau.
- Cycles and executions are redundant restatements of the stopping round.
- Token differences to target (−1.2k original, −3.9k variant) hinge on single runs, with fully overlapping ranges and inconsistent total-spend direction.
- A round-1 arm imbalance in tokens and coverage profile is present before any feedback could act.

Treat this as a feasibility pilot only. Before any effect claim, the following are needed:
- raw-artifact verification of the cross-version duplicate trajectories and the round-1 imbalance,
- a toggle-reachability audit,
- a nonzero defect or mutation signal,
- substantially more independent sessions or IPs.
