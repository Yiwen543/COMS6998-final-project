## Answers to the blind review's open questions

- **Residual 2 branches and 2 expressions:** these are lines 118 and 185. They can only fire if `OPT_WRITE_ON_FULL` or `OPT_READ_ON_EMPTY` is set, and both are 0 here. That makes them almost certainly unreachable; I inferred this from the source and did not prove it.
- **Z's (P's) extra requests:** they are by design. They are not counted in tokens but are counted in wall time. P's wall time also includes about 4 seconds of token counting and about 0.3 seconds of e-graph setup (C pays the setup too). Without the counting time, P's wall times are 29.9 / 26.5 / 40.5 seconds.
- **Run order:** arms ran one after another (A, then B, P, C), not interleaved. Repetitions 2–3 hit the prompt cache in round 1. Wall time is therefore confounded and should not be read as an arm effect.

## Interpretation

- **C vs B:** no difference in target round, and C cost about 6.5k more tokens. B's raw list already shows the source text for lines 91, 96, 161 and 179. C only adds that they pair up, and equivalent guards fire together anyway. On a raw list this short, grouping is of little use. A faster C was possible only at round 4, which is the 32-cycle floor; every arm finished at round 5 at best.
- **C vs P:** at round 5, C reached the target in 3/3 sessions and P in 2/3, at matched input tokens. The one late P session started round 2 with a reset, which cost it a round. One session out of three is not evidence that the e-graph content helped.
- **A vs the coverage-feedback arms:** this is the only consistent gap (A always round 6; B/P/C round 5 in 8 of 9). The cause is visible in the logs: A never drained with 8 pure reads in round 4.
- **Repetitions:** outputs repeat within arms and across arms, so there are effectively far fewer than three independent runs per arm.
- **Scope:** this is a synthetic mechanism test of one FIFO variant, not a second natural benchmark. It says nothing about generalization.
